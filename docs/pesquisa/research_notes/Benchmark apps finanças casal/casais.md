# Couple/household money features: app benchmark and evidence (2024–2026)

> Method note: WebFetch could not reach any page (DNS errors on monarch.com, bankrate.com, tecnoblog.net), so every finding below comes from search-result summaries of the cited pages. I could not read full articles. Treat exact numbers as "reported by" and check the primary page before quoting it publicly. Older (pre-2024) items are marked **[older]**.

## Q1. Which couple- and household-specific features exist across apps? (catalogue)

### Takeaway
The market has settled on a set of features: per-partner logins, per-account visibility controls, and a split between shared and individual views. On top of that, apps offer split/settle-up tools (equal, custom, or income-based ratios), comments on transactions, bill reminders assigned to a person, shared goals, and real-time spend notifications. The newest notable launch is Monarch's "yours / mine / ours" ownership labels (Oct 2025), which is almost exactly the "meu / dela / nosso" model. Income-based (proportional) splitting is mostly found in small niche apps, not in the big ones.

### Cited Findings
**Ownership labels and joint vs. individual views**
- Monarch launched **Shared Views** on Oct 30, 2025. It lets couples see personal and household finances side by side using "yours, mine, and ours" labels. — [Monarch What's New](https://www.monarch.com/blog/shared-views)
- Monarch: accounts and individual transactions can each be tagged with an owner. By default a transaction inherits its account's owner. You can override a single transaction or create rules for certain kinds of transactions. Existing accounts default to "Shared". Filters switch between personal, partner, and household views across accounts, net worth, transactions, and reports. The feature is only available to households with multiple members. — [Monarch Help: Shared Views](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/42228648365076-Shared-Views-in-Monarch)
- Monarch: you can invite a partner at no extra cost, and the partner gets a separate login. — [Monarch for Couples](https://www.monarchmoney.com/for-couples)
- Zeta **[older, 2021–2023]**: shared and individual budgets, and finances shown across "shared and individual pools of money". You can tag a partner on a transaction or split a purchase. Each member controls 100% of how much they share. Bill Reserve for shared expenses, and savings sub-accounts for goals. — [College Investor Zeta review](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/24184/zeta-review/); [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/09/zeta-joint-card-seed/)
- Goodbudget: household budget syncs across devices and the partner sees spending in real time. The forum recommends "Envelope Groups", for example "Shared: Groceries" next to "Personal: Groceries". The free tier is limited to 20 envelopes, and bank sync is paid. — [Goodbudget envelope budgeting](https://goodbudget.com/envelope-budgeting); [Goodbudget forum](https://forums.goodbudget.com/t/separate-personal-and-family-budgets/3799)
- YNAB Together: up to 6 members share budgets, and each member has their own account and login. — [YNAB Together](https://www.ynab.com/ynab-together)

**Privacy controls (the "private personal spending" problem)**
- Honeydue: per account, each partner chooses to share balances and transactions, share balances only, or hide the account entirely. One source says individual transactions can also be hidden. — [FinCompareLab Honeydue review](https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/honeydue-review/); [Clark.com](https://clark.com/personal-finance-credit/budgeting-saving/honeydue/)
- A comparison site says Honeydue handles partial privacy best and that YNAB and Monarch are "closer to all-or-nothing". This conflicts with a Gerald blog claiming YNAB members choose what to show. Both are secondary sources with commercial interests. — [FinCompareLab](https://www.fincomparelab.com/?p=558); [Gerald YNAB Together guide](https://joingerald.com/learn/financial-wellness/ynab-together-guide)
- Copilot Money has no household tier. Partner sharing works through a Magic Link login, and the help page warns that any additional logged-in device gets full control of the account. An open feature request asks for shared budgets between two accounts. — [Copilot Help](https://intercom.help/copilotmoney/en/articles/4523792-sharing-your-account-with-a-partner); [Copilot roadmap request](https://roadmap.copilot.money/feature-requests/p/couplefamily-support-shared-budgets)
- DuoDivvy (niche): income-proportional splits plus "private personal dashboards" with a joint overview, without merging bank accounts. — [AlternativeTo DuoDivvy](https://alternativeto.net/software/duodivvy/about)
- **No source found** for an app that shows the partner's personal spending as category totals only, which is the target app's design. The closest options are Honeydue's "balance only" setting and Zeta's "how much to share" control.

**Splitting, IOUs and settle-up**
- Honeydue: partners can split expenses directly in the app. — [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/best-budgeting-apps-for-couples/)
- Tandem (launched Aug 2023, $12/month per couple): links accounts, keeps a ledger of individual purchases, lets partners split transactions, and supports custom split ratios based on income. — [TechCrunch Jan 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/17/tandem-modern-couples-app-fintech); [Motley Fool](https://fool.com/the-ascent/personal-finance/articles/could-this-personal-finance-app-for-couples-save-your-relationship/)
- Splitwise supports equal, unequal, and percentage splits, so income ratios have to be entered by hand. Settle Up supports equal, unequal, and recurring splits. No major splitter has a built-in "split by income" mode. — [IONOS bill-splitting guide](https://www.ionos.com/digitalguide/online-marketing/web-analytics/best-bill-splitting-apps.md)
- Tricount's couples blog describes even or uneven splits. — [Tricount blog](https://tricount.com/blog/how-tricount-makes-managing-couple-expenses-easy)
- Newer small apps (2025–2026 App Store listings) offer income tracking that computes each partner's "fair share", a toggle between fair and equal splits, and **per-expense split overrides** that leave the default unchanged. — [Couples Budget Tracker (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6751105610); [CoupleSplit (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/it/app/couplesplit/id6759464915)

**Communication: comments, reactions, chat, notifications**
- Honeydue: the Activity tab lets you comment on and react to transactions, and a Chat tab handles money discussions. — [Clark.com](https://clark.com/personal-finance-credit/budgeting-saving/honeydue/)
- Honeydue: monthly category limits, with alerts when you or your partner approach a limit. — [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/best-budgeting-apps-for-couples/)
- Honeydue bill reminders: you set amount, due date, type, and recurrence, and choose who gets reminded (me, partner, or both), up to a week ahead. The app prompts you to add common bills (rent, phone, electricity). — [Clark.com](https://clark.com/personal-finance-credit/budgeting-saving/honeydue/); [College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/35477/honeydue-review/)
- Noh (Brazil): real-time notifications of every purchase to both partners. — [Noh, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noh-conta-conjunta-do-casal/id1609125328)

**Shared goals and savings**
- Tandem launched a "Goals" feature (Apr 2024) for jointly saving toward big purchases, with interest. — [American Banker / CFP Board](https://www.cfp.net/news/2024/04/relationship-finance-app-tandem-debuts-new-goals-feature)
- Noh: shared savings boxes ("caixinhas compartilhadas"). — [Noh, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noh-conta-conjunta-do-casal/id1609125328)

**Approval requests / allowance**
- No source found for an "approval request" feature (asking a partner to approve a purchase) in any app reviewed. Per-person "fun money" appears as advice in guides (see Q3), but I found no named app with a dedicated allowance feature.

**Brazil: joint-account fintechs and local apps**
- Noh: a digital joint account for couples. Equal access for both, Pix, two cards with a single shared credit card bill and a joint limit, shared savings boxes, and real-time notifications. — [Noh, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noh-conta-conjunta-do-casal/id1609125328); [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/noh-conta-conjunta-e-cartao/1609125328)
- Nubank: an undated guide says Nubank has no joint account. A Tecnoblog headline mentions "Nubank lança saldo compartilhado para clientes Ultravioleta", but I could not read the body to confirm date or details. — [Melhores Cartões](https://www.melhorescartoes.com.br/conta-cartao-nubank-vale-a-pena-2025.html); [Tecnoblog](https://tecnoblog.net/comunidade/t/nubank-lanca-saldo-compartilhado-para-clientes-ultravioleta/89396)
- Inter: no 2025 source found on a joint account. An undated forum post says neither Inter nor Nubank offers one, per their support chats. — [Tecnoblog forum](https://tecnoblog.net/comunidade/t/conta-conjunta-gratuita-banco-inter-em-2-apple-pay/46777)
- Mobills is described as letting you create shared accounts and sync to the cloud with the same login on several devices (shared login, not separate partner logins). The Mobills article is from around 2023. — [Mobills blog](https://www.mobills.com.br/blog/educacao-financeira/financas-compartilhadas/); [Monitor do Mercado](https://monitordomercado.com.br/noticias/271743-os-melhores-aplicativos-para-casais-controlarem-o-dinheiro-juntos/)
- Organizze: a Tecnoblog community user could not find a shared-wallet feature. — [Tecnoblog forum](https://tecnoblog.net/comunidade/t/app-gerenciamento-financeiro/6032.md)

### Inferences
- "meu / dela / nosso" has a strong market precedent in Monarch's yours/mine/ours (2025). The useful mechanics to copy are: the owner is set on the account, transactions inherit it by default, single transactions can be overridden, and rules can be set by type.
- Showing a partner's private spending as category totals only looks unoccupied among the apps reviewed. It sits between Honeydue's "balance only" and full sharing, and could be a differentiator.
- In Brazil, the space looks thin. Noh is the only couple-native joint account found, the big banks seem to lack joint accounts (unconfirmed), and local trackers (Mobills, Organizze) rely on shared logins. A couple-native tracker with separate logins and privacy layers seems to have little direct local competition.
- Income-proportional splitting with per-expense overrides is a clear feature to build. Incumbents make users do the math by hand.

### Gaps
- Could not get feature details for Plenty, Buddy, Ivy, or Fairshare. Searches returned nothing substantive.
- Zeta's current status in 2025–2026 is unverified. Sources are 2021–2023 and no shutdown news was found.
- I could not confirm whether Monarch lets a partner hide accounts under Shared Views (full help page unreachable).
- No Brazilian couples-tracker app with separate logins and privacy controls was found. The search may not have been exhaustive (e.g., smaller App Store / Play apps).

## Q2. Which features are most used or praised, and which drew complaints?

### Takeaway
Hard usage data is almost absent. What exists points to per-account privacy controls and comments/chat (Honeydue) as the features most praised as couple-specific. Separate logins at no extra cost (Monarch) are a recurring selling point. The complaints concentrate on bank-sync reliability, unanswered support / stalled development (Honeydue), and splitter paywalls (Splitwise daily cap). Not one couple-specific feature drew complaints of its own. Most frustration comes from data reliability and pricing.

### Cited Findings
- Honeydue: app-store and Reddit reviews report sync delays, missing transactions, and bugs. A bank-sync fix shipped on iOS in June 2026 but reportedly not clearly on Android (single source). A pattern of unanswered support requests in 2025–2026, and "no significant new features in recent memory". — [FinCompareLab Honeydue review](https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/honeydue-review/); [Gerald Honeydue review](https://joingerald.com/learn/money-basics/honeydue-app-review)
- Honeydue rating figures conflict. One site calls a 3.4-star Google Play figure stale and gives 4.3/5. An aggregator's NLP shows about 65% positive. — [FinCompareLab](https://www.fincomparelab.com/reviews/honeydue-review/); [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1157633945/honeydue-couples-finance/reviews)
- An older Honeydue review notes buggy behavior and transactions not showing up right away **[older]**. — [College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/35477/honeydue-review/)
- Splitwise: around Dec 31, 2023 the free tier was capped at 3–4 expenses per day (reports vary). Users complained about poor communication and Pro pricing (about $3.99–$8/month), and complaints continued into May 2024. Sources are Trustpilot plus competitor/report sites. — [Trustpilot (NO)](https://no.trustpilot.com/review/splitwise.com?page=2); [Kimola report](https://kimola.com/reports/splitwise-app-feedback-report-uncover-user-insights-google-play-en-144452)
- In one couples-app roundup, bank-sync reliability is the most common complaint from couples. — [Gerald YNAB Together guide](https://joingerald.com/learn/financial-wellness/ynab-together-guide)
- Copilot users opened a feature request for shared budgets between two accounts, which shows demand for proper household support. — [Copilot roadmap](https://feedback.copilot.money/feature-requests/p/couplefamily-support-shared-budgets)
- Third-party analysis (Sacra) frames shared household use of Monarch as creating "household lock-in", meaning two-person usage raises retention. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/d8600f0b-b848-475c-ab78-098f71bcf66f/)
- Copilot's sharing model gives any extra device full control of the account, which is a privacy weakness for couples. — [Copilot Help](https://intercom.help/copilotmoney/en/articles/4523792-sharing-your-account-with-a-partner)

### Inferences
- For a pt-BR app, reliable data entry or import (Open Finance or fast manual entry) matters as much as couple features. Sync failure is the top cause of churn across apps.
- Avoid usage caps on core logging (the Splitwise backlash). Monetize extras instead, and don't charge for the second partner (Monarch's free partner seat is praised).
- Developer neglect is a known weakness of Honeydue, the leading free couples app. Visible, steady improvement can be a competitive edge.

### Gaps
- No source gives usage rates per feature (e.g., % of couples using comments, goals, or settle-up). Reddit threads did not come up in searches (r/MonarchMoney, r/ynab, r/personalfinance were not reachable), so first-hand user feedback on Monarch Shared Views is missing.
- No review data found for Noh, Mobills, or Organizze couple features.

## Q3. What do couple-finance experts and research recommend that an app can operationalize? (incl. causes of money conflict)

### Takeaway
The evidence and expert advice line up on three points. First, transparency and a "we" framing improve relationships (Olson et al., 2023 RCT on joint accounts). Second, secrecy is common and seen as a serious breach (Bankrate: about 40% admit financial infidelity). Third, the most-recommended structure is a hybrid "yours / mine / ours", with shared costs split proportionally to income when incomes differ, guilt-free personal "fun money", and a short monthly money date. Brazilian surveys show roughly 4 to 5 in 10 couples fight about money, mainly over household spending.

### Cited Findings
**Research on conflict and transparency**
- Olson et al., *Journal of Consumer Research* (2023): 230 engaged or newlywed couples were randomly assigned to a joint account or kept separate accounts, and followed for 2 years. The joint-account group reported higher relationship satisfaction, linked to goal alignment, transparency, and a communal ("we") view. Separate-account couples framed money as an exchange. About 20% attrition, and the sample was not representative. — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/05/230504111937.htm); [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/key-to-happy-marriage-joint-bank-account) **[older, 2023, still the key study]**
- Bankrate (Jan 2025): 40% of Americans in committed relationships admit financial infidelity toward their current partner. Overspending is the most common type. — [Bankrate 2025](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/news/financial-infidelity-survey-2025)
- Bankrate/YouGov (Dec 2025): 43% say keeping financial secrets is at least as bad as physical infidelity, and almost half of married or cohabiting couples don't share all their financial details. — [Bankrate press release](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/43-believe-financial-infidelity-is-at-least-as-bad-as-physical-cheating/)
- Bankrate 2024 data: 67% of Gen Z in relationships report some financial infidelity, and 88% of Gen Z couples keep separate accounts (as summarized in search results). — [Bankrate press release](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/43-believe-financial-infidelity-is-at-least-as-bad-as-physical-cheating/)
- Money Mentors (Canada, Feb 2025): 47% have had financial disagreements with their partner, and 1 in 10 considered separating because of finances. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250213231064/en/Survey-Half-of-Canadian-Couples-Who-Fight-Over-Money-Lose-Sleep)
- Jeffrey Dew (Utah State): couples who disagree about money almost daily are 69% more likely to divorce. This comes from a secondary source with no date. **[older]** — [ConsumerCredit](https://www.consumercredit.com/?p=2522)

**Brazil-specific conflict data**
- SPC Brasil/CNDL: 39% of married or cohabiting respondents argue about money. The triggers are household spending (41%), no emergency savings (32%), and refusing to pay the partner's expenses (19%). A Nubank blog cites 48% from the same research, so the figures conflict. The year is unconfirmed. — [Monitor Mercantil](https://monitormercantil.com.br/quatro-em-cada-10-brasileiros-casados-brigam-com-parceiro-por-causa-de-dinheiro/); [Nubank blog](https://blog.nubank.com.br/por-que-casais-brigam-sobre-dinheiro/)
- Meu Compromisso + B3: 36% of couples argue about money at least weekly. — [Bem Paraná](https://www.bemparana.com.br/noticias/economia/36-dos-casais-brigam-sobre-dinheiro-pelo-menos-uma-vez-por-semana-diz-pesquisa/)
- Serasa: 53% consider money the main reason couples fight. The Serasa blog also covers CPF negativado (credit blacklisting) caused by a relationship. — [Fast Company Brasil](https://fastcompanybrasil.com/money/veja-como-um-casal-pode-cuidar-das-financas-e-melhorar-a-relacao/); [Serasa blog](https://www.serasa.com.br/blog/dinheiro-e-relacionamento-como-evitar-conflitos-financeiros-no-amor/)

**Expert systems to operationalize**
- Hybrid yours/mine/ours (one joint account for shared bills and goals plus a personal account for each partner) is favored by Nicole Lapin. She argues that what predicts success is both partners seeing the full picture and talking regularly, more than the account structure itself. — [Nicole Lapin](https://nicolelapin.com/articles/couples-combine-finances)
- Monthly money date of 20–30 minutes (some couples prefer every two weeks). — [Nicole Lapin](https://nicolelapin.com/articles/couples-combine-finances); [Utah Financial Wellness 2023](https://financialwellness.utah.edu/blog/posts/2023/october2023blogrelationships.php)
- Money-date agenda: review the last 2–4 weeks of spending, catch double charges, cancel unused subscriptions, check balances against goals, and build a bill calendar for next month. — [Wealthtender checklist](https://wealthtender.com/insights/the-money-date-a-simple-checklist-for-couples-who-want-to-get-on-the-same-page-financially/)
- Proportional splitting is called "the most equitable" by many couples. Example: a 70% earner covers 70% of joint bills, so both keep a similar share of income. The same guide recommends separate guilt-free fun money so the budget isn't abandoned. — [PocketGuard](https://pocketguard.com/blog/budgeting-for-couples/)
- Brazilian guides give the same advice. Proportional is the most balanced model when incomes differ, and 50/50 works when incomes are similar. Example: with salaries of R$6k and R$3k and bills of R$4.5k, a 50/50 split leaves one partner R$750 and the other R$3,750. Another worked example: rendas de R$3k/R$7k → R$2.1k (30%) / R$4.9k (70%). Alternatives are splitting by responsibility (each partner owns certain bills) or combining with a joint account. — [Guardar Dinheiro](https://www.guardardinheiro.com.br/blog/como-dividir-as-contas-do-casal-modelos); [InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/minhas-financas/planejamento-financeiro-como-organizar-as-financas-do-casal/amp/); [Serasa](https://www.serasa.com.br/score/blog/como-dividir-as-contas-do-casal/); [C6 Bank blog](https://www.c6bank.com.br/blog/como-dividir-as-contas-do-casal)
- Brazilian legal and finance blog advice: one individual account each plus a joint account for shared expenses "preserves independence and reduces friction". — [AMD Jus](https://amdjus.com.br/desvendando-os-beneficios-e-armadilhas-da-conta-conjunta-para-casais/)

### Inferences
Mapping advice to app features for the "meu / dela / nosso" app:
- **Proportional split by income**: a configurable ratio (50/50, by income, or custom) applied to "nosso" expenses, with a monthly "quem deve a quem" (who owes whom) settle-up and per-expense overrides. This directly addresses the top Brazilian trigger, disputes over household spending.
- **Category-total privacy for "meu / dela"**: this meets the research's transparency goal (each partner sees where money goes at a high level) without enabling the surveillance that drives secrecy. It is a middle ground between full sharing and the hidden accounts that make up "financial infidelity".
- **Fun money per partner**: a personal monthly allowance shown to the other partner only as "usado X de Y", consistent with the category-total privacy.
- **Money date ritual**: a monthly guided review screen (last month's "nosso" spending vs. plan, new subscriptions or duplicate charges, goal progress, next month's bills calendar) with a reminder notification. It should take 20–30 minutes.
- **Shared goals ("caixinhas do casal")** framed as "nós" to build the communal identity that Olson et al. tie to satisfaction.
- **Comments/reactions on "nosso" transactions** and bill reminders assigned to a person (Honeydue's most distinctive features).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that tests app-mediated rituals (e.g., money dates) or proportional splitting specifically. These are expert and blog recommendations.
- Nath Finanças and Me Poupe! content on couples did not come up in searches. A direct YouTube or Instagram review would be needed.
- The years for the Brazilian conflict surveys (SPC/CNDL, Meu Compromisso/B3, Serasa) could not be confirmed, and the SPC/CNDL figures conflict (39% vs 48%).
